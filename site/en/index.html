Can a mathematical proof be repeated the way a software test is repeated?

This year, OpenAI published computer-verified proofs about the Navier–Stokes equations, which describe how fluids move. I'm a software quality assurance (QA) engineer, not a mathematician. I reviewed that work the way I review software, and I left everything ready for anyone to repeat it. I'm grateful to anyone who takes the time to try it. I'd like many people, on many different computers, to repeat it.

Try it on your computer

I put together a check that works on Windows, Mac and Linux. It takes under a minute and installs nothing on your machine besides Python.

  1. Install Python 3.9 or newer

    From python.org. If you already have it, move on.

  2. Download the repository

    Download the ZIP and unzip it. You'll get a folder named scientific-qa-evidence-main.

  3. Run a single command

    Open a terminal inside that folder and type:

    python verificar.py

    On Mac or Linux it may be python3 verificar.py. You'll see OK or FAIL on six checks, plus your fingerprint.

  4. Tell us what you got

    With the review and reproduction form (you need a free GitHub account). Paste the contents of mi_resultado.txt and, if you like, attach screenshots or a video. A different result is just as useful.

What this is and isn't

What it is

  • The software-quality review I did of proofs OpenAI published.
  • The public record I kept, with every command, version, hash and result, including the errors I fixed.
  • A check that this record gives the same result on several computers.

What it isn't

  • The solution to a Millennium Problem. The mathematics is OpenAI's, and the classical version of the problem, with no external force, is still open.
  • A certification or a peer review.
  • An independent rebuild of the Lean proof by someone else. That is still pending.

My other experiment: a physics debugger for an AI predictor

Here I'm not reviewing anyone else's work. I trained a neural network that predicts how a fluid moves in 3D, and I wrote a debugger that checks whether each prediction respects physics: that the fluid doesn't compress, that energy adds up, and that the evolution obeys the equations. I tested it over four rounds. In each one I committed the hypotheses and thresholds before generating the test data, and the result was the same on three computers. One of the hypotheses failed, and I report it that way.

I'm looking for someone to prepare sabotaged predictions, blind, for round 5: I won't know which ones are traps until the verdicts are published.
