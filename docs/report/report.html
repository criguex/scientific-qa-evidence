Scientific QA Evidence

An independent QA audit of OpenAI's Lean proofs for Navier–Stokes alternatives (C) and (D) and the Euler singularity, with Lean bridges to Clay's text and checker controls · Report for release v1.3.1 · 22 October 2026
Author: Cristian Guerra, QA Automation, Medellín, Colombia · github.com/criguex/scientific-qa-evidence
Read this first. The proofs belong to OpenAI (openai/NavierStokesAndEuler). This work proves nothing new, claims no Millennium Prize result and is not a mathematical review. It is a software-QA study: rebuild the published artifacts, recheck them with independent checkers, test whether the checking pipeline itself catches errors, and publish every step with hashes.

1. Conclusions

  1. OpenAI's (C), (D) and Euler proofs check, repeatedly and from scratch. Comparator, with nanoda and the Lean kernel, accepted both challenges on five different builds, the last two compiled entirely from source with nothing prebuilt (lake --no-cache build, exit 0, 11,424 jobs, 1 h 29 min and 1 h 16 min). A fresh leanchecker replay agreed, and the kernel source used includes every known 2026 kernel soundness fix.
  2. Comparator checks the exact statement. It refused all six one-symbol changes to the challenge and accepted a comment-only control. The challenge's 18 declarations match Google DeepMind's public Formal Conjectures statement after textual normalization (name resolution not verified).
  3. OpenAI's theorems imply Clay's (C) and (D) as written literally in Lean, for every ν > 0, with no hypotheses and only the standard axioms, under both readings of smoothness at t = 0 (k1_C, k1_D, k1_C_oneSided, k1_D_oneSided). This is relative to the literal rendering ClayLiteral, which nobody independent has reviewed against the PDF; (D) reads the erratum on periodic pressure as a solution condition, and (C) does not use it.
  4. A refusal is not a refutation. On three Mathlib theorems, Comparator refused all eight changed statements, including four equivalent ones, one of them definitionally equal. A second route in Lean, with a guard against using the theorem under test, classified each as equivalent, weaker or false, all as pre-registered.
  5. Not settled here. Whether the Lean proof follows OpenAI's paper is out of scope and is disputed by Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen (arXiv:2610.08144). No mathematician has reviewed the bridges.
  6. What the protocol adds. In this case it surfaced eight findings that the documented verification procedure does not produce, three of them corrected defects in this study's own work. In a pre-registered benchmark its checks flagged four planted fault classes that the baseline passes. Four review rounds found and fixed 17 issues in that experiment itself.
  7. Not claimed. Methodological novelty (a targeted literature search found close prior work), a real-world detection rate, or any part of the mathematical proof.

3. Numerical mutation campaign

Nine synthetic models × three configurations, independent oracles, frozen contract (tolerance 10−6). Per host: 27 PASS, 54 KILLED, 3 SURVIVED, identical on macOS arm64 and Linux x86_64 (maximum scaled difference 1.11×10−16). The 54 KILLED are 51 distinct behaviors; the 3 survivors are a deliberately tiny RC change and stay visible. A rerun reproduces all 84 saved records. Synthetic models, not physical experiments.

6. From OpenAI's theorem to Clay's text

To get Clay's (C) and (D) from OpenAI's formal theorem, one direction per clause is enough: OpenAI's data must meet Clay's conditions, and any Clay solution must be a formal solution. Clay's equations (1)–(11), (C), (D) and the erratum are written literally in Lean, in coordinates and with one-sided time derivatives (ClayLiteral.lean); Bridges.lean proves each clause and composes them.

Every step has a receipt with command, input and output hashes and exit code; verify.py checks the records against them and has regression tests for two verifier defects found by an outside reviewer. Limits: author-designed cases, no held-out cases yet, three theorems only.

9. Reproducibility and traceability

  • Release v1.3.1, content commit __COMMIT__; CI green on Ubuntu and macOS, Python 3.9 and 3.12. An independent automated audit of the READMEs against the records was run before this release, and its confirmed findings were fixed.

A fifth build, B5, repeated the full build from source and Comparator accepted both challenges again (evidence/formal/b5/).
