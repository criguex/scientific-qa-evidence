Scientific QA Evidence

An independent QA reproduction of OpenAI's Lean proofs for Navier–Stokes alternatives (C) and (D) and the Euler singularity · Report for release v1.2.0 + bridge plan (commit c400a6b) · 21 October 2026
Author: Cristian Guerra, QA Automation, Medellín, Colombia · github.com/criguex/scientific-qa-evidence
Read this first. The proofs belong to OpenAI (openai/NavierStokesAndEuler). This work proves nothing new, claims no Millennium Prize result and is not a mathematical review. It is a software-QA study: rebuild the published artifacts, recheck them with independent checkers, test whether the checking pipeline itself catches errors, and publish every step with hashes.

1. Conclusions

  1. OpenAI's (C), (D) and Euler proofs check, repeatedly and from scratch. Comparator, with nanoda and the Lean kernel, accepted both challenges on four different builds, the last compiled entirely from source with nothing prebuilt (lake --no-cache build, exit 0, 11,424 jobs, 1 h 29 min). A fresh leanchecker replay agreed, and the kernel source used includes every known 2026 kernel soundness fix.
  2. Comparator checks the exact statement. It refused all six one-symbol changes to the challenge and accepted a comment-only control. The challenge's 18 declarations match Google DeepMind's public Formal Conjectures statement after textual normalization (name resolution not verified).
  3. Not settled here. Whether the formal statement says exactly what Clay's text says: a reading-based review leaves one interpretive point (periodic pressure in D) and a semantic work plan with 18 bridge claims, none yet formally proved or independently reviewed. Whether the Lean proof follows OpenAI's paper is out of scope and is disputed by arXiv:2610.08144.
  4. What the protocol adds. In this case it surfaced eight findings that the documented verification procedure does not produce, three of them corrected defects in this study's own work. In a pre-registered benchmark its checks flagged four planted fault classes that the baseline passes. Four review rounds found and fixed 17 issues in that experiment itself.
  5. Not claimed. Methodological novelty (a targeted literature search found close prior work), a real-world detection rate, or any part of the mathematical proof.

3. Numerical mutation campaign

Nine synthetic models × three configurations, independent oracles, frozen contract (tolerance 10−6). Per host: 27 PASS, 54 KILLED, 3 SURVIVED, identical on macOS arm64 and Linux x86_64 (maximum scaled difference 1.11×10−16). The 54 KILLED are 51 distinct behaviors; the 3 survivors are a deliberately tiny RC change and stay visible. A rerun reproduces all 84 saved records. Synthetic models, not physical experiments.
